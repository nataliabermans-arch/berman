Votiva is a radiofrequency-based treatment system that has been used in women's intimate wellness care. It is designed to deliver controlled energy to selected tissues, depending on the handpiece and treatment approach. Women researching the Votiva vaginal rejuvenation treatment may want to understand how the technology works, which areas it addresses, what a session involves, and what the available medical evidence says.

Although Votiva is often discussed as a non-surgical option, it is important to distinguish between the device's technology, its intended uses, and claims made about vaginal rejuvenation. Radiofrequency energy has been studied for several vulvovaginal applications, but the evidence does not establish that every advertised benefit is guaranteed or that the treatment is appropriate for every patient.

Here are five important things to understand before considering Votiva.

## Short Summary

- Votiva is an energy-based treatment system that uses radiofrequency technology.
- Depending on the handpiece and treatment plan, the system may be used for selected internal or external tissue applications.
- Treatment duration and the number of sessions depend on the device configuration, provider's protocol, and individual circumstances.
- Research on radiofrequency treatments for vaginal rejuvenation has limitations, and long-term outcomes are not fully established.
- FDA safety guidance and individual medical assessment are important when evaluating any energy-based vaginal procedure.

### 1. Votiva Uses Radiofrequency Energy to Treat Selected Tissues

The first thing to understand about the Votiva vaginal rejuvenation treatment is that it uses radiofrequency (RF) energy rather than laser energy or surgical techniques. Radiofrequency produces controlled heat within targeted tissue. The Votiva system was developed by InMode and includes different handpieces designed for different treatment applications.

The FormaV handpiece uses radiofrequency energy to deliver controlled heating. Another handpiece historically associated with the Votiva platform, FractoraV, uses fractional radiofrequency technology. These technologies do not work in exactly the same way, so it is important to know which device and handpiece a provider is proposing to use.

### How Does Radiofrequency Technology Work?

Radiofrequency energy generates heat in the targeted tissue. In aesthetic medicine, controlled thermal stimulation is used in certain procedures intended to affect tissue structure and collagen-related processes.

Collagen is a structural protein that helps provide support to skin and other connective tissues. Changes in collagen and tissue elasticity can occur with aging and hormonal transitions. However, the fact that radiofrequency can produce thermal effects does not automatically prove that a particular treatment will restore vaginal function, improve sexual sensation, or resolve a medical condition.

The treatment's actual purpose, expected outcome, and supporting evidence should be explained by the treating physician.

### 2. Votiva Can Involve Different Internal and External Treatment Approaches

A second important fact is that Votiva is not limited to one treatment area. Depending on the device configuration and clinical plan, treatment may involve internal vaginal tissue, external vulvar tissue, or a combination of areas.

The internal vaginal canal and external vulva are different anatomical structures. They have different tissue characteristics and may require different treatment approaches. This is why a provider should explain exactly which area is being considered and which handpiece will be used.

### Internal Treatment

Internal treatment refers to applying the appropriate device handpiece within the vaginal canal. The treatment is intended to deliver controlled radiofrequency energy to selected vaginal tissue.

Before recommending an internal procedure, a clinician should review the patient's symptoms, medical history, examination findings, and treatment goals. A general description of vaginal laxity or dryness is not enough to establish that an energy-based procedure is medically appropriate.

### External Treatment

External treatment refers to selected areas of the vulva, which include the labia and surrounding external genital structures. The specific approach depends on the technology being used and the treatment plan.

Not every Votiva configuration or handpiece is interchangeable. Patients should ask whether the proposed procedure is intended for internal tissue, external tissue, or both, and why that approach is being recommended.

Understanding this distinction helps prevent confusion between different procedures that are sometimes marketed under the broad term vaginal rejuvenation.

### 3. Treatment Sessions Are Individualized, Not One-Size-Fits-All

A third thing to know about Votiva is that treatment duration and the number of sessions can vary. There is no single session schedule that should be presented as appropriate for every woman.

Some descriptions of Votiva protocols refer to multiple sessions, while the actual plan depends on the treatment configuration, clinical assessment, and the provider's protocol. A physician should explain the proposed schedule before treatment begins rather than promising a particular number of sessions or a guaranteed result.

### What Happens During a Consultation?

A consultation should begin with a discussion of the patient's concerns and expectations. The provider may ask about relevant medical history, previous procedures, medications, hormonal changes, and any symptoms involving the vaginal or vulvar area.

The clinician should also determine whether an examination or further evaluation is needed before considering treatment.

Useful questions to ask include:

- Which Votiva handpiece will be used?
- Is the proposed treatment internal, external, or both?
- What specific concern is the procedure intended to address?
- How many sessions are being proposed, and why?
- What evidence supports the expected outcome?
- What are the possible risks and alternatives?

### 4. Expected Results Should Be Based on Evidence, Not Marketing Promises

A fourth important fact is that claims about Votiva's results need to be evaluated carefully. Patients may encounter descriptions suggesting improvements in tissue laxity, lubrication, sensation, or intimate comfort. However, these outcomes should not be presented as certain or guaranteed.

Research into radiofrequency-based treatments for vulvovaginal concerns has reported some potentially positive findings. Nevertheless, studies vary in design, patient populations, treatment protocols, and follow-up periods. Results from one radiofrequency device or study cannot automatically be applied to every Votiva handpiece or treatment protocol.

### What Does Clinical Research Say?

A review published in the Journal of Sexual Medicine examined radiofrequency-based devices for female genitourinary indications. The researchers identified substantial variation in the available evidence and concluded that further well-designed randomized controlled trials were needed to establish more decisive recommendations about safety and effectiveness.

You can read the research in [Radiofrequency-Based Devices](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32129169/) for Female Genito-Urinary Indications: Position Statements From the European Society of Sexual Medicine.

This distinction matters because early or limited research does not establish a treatment as a proven solution for every condition. It is also important not to confuse patient satisfaction, short-term symptom changes, and demonstrated long-term medical benefit.

### Why Realistic Expectations Matter

Before choosing Votiva, patients should understand that:

- Individual responses to radiofrequency treatment may differ.
- A reported improvement in one symptom does not establish improvement in every aspect of sexual or urinary health.
- Long-term durability and the need for maintenance are not fully established for every application.
- Treatment should not be described as a guaranteed alternative to surgery or established medical care.

If a provider recommends Votiva, ask what specific outcome is realistic in your situation and what evidence supports that expectation.

### 5. Safety, FDA Guidance, and Medical Evaluation Matter Before Treatment

The fifth and perhaps most important thing to understand about Votiva is that a non-surgical procedure is not automatically risk-free. Energy-based devices can produce thermal effects, and their safety depends on the device, intended use, treatment settings, operator training, and the patient's medical circumstances.

Patients should not rely solely on promotional descriptions such as painless, safe, or FDA-approved. Regulatory clearance for a device or handpiece for a specific indication does not mean that it has been approved for every advertised vaginal rejuvenation purpose.

### What Has the FDA Said About Energy-Based Vaginal Procedures?

The FDA issued a safety communication on July 30, 2018, warning patients and healthcare professionals about energy-based devices marketed for vaginal rejuvenation and vaginal cosmetic procedures.

The communication explained that the safety and effectiveness of these devices for treating vaginal laxity, dryness, menopausal symptoms, urinary incontinence, and sexual-function concerns had not been established. It also identified reports of serious complications, including burns, scarring, pain during intercourse, and recurring or chronic pain.

You can review the [FDA Safety Communication](https://www.fda.gov/MedicalDevices/) on Energy-Based Vaginal Rejuvenation Devices.

It is important to understand the regulatory distinction: a device may have clearance for a particular medical or general-use indication without having FDA clearance or approval for vaginal rejuvenation. Patients should ask the provider to explain the exact regulatory status and intended use of the device being offered.

### What Additional Safety Information Should Patients Know?

Regulatory concerns have also continued internationally. In April 2026, Australia's Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) reported that its review of energy-based devices used for vaginal rejuvenation found insufficient clinical evidence to support their therapeutic use and long-term safety. The agency stated that devices intended for this purpose had been cancelled from the Australian Register of Therapeutic Goods.

This is an Australian regulatory decision, not a statement that every device has the same regulatory status in every country. However, it reinforces why patients should examine current evidence and local regulatory information rather than relying on marketing claims.

The [TGA's findings are available in its Post-Market Review](https://www.tga.gov.au/safety/safety-monitoring-and-information/medical-device-post-market-monitoring-and-safety-updates/post-market-review-energy-based-devices-used-vaginal-rejuvenation) of Energy-Based Devices Used for Vaginal Rejuvenation.

### Why a Medical Assessment Is Essential

A qualified clinician should review your health history, symptoms, examination findings, and treatment goals before recommending an energy-based procedure. Patients should also receive a clear explanation of potential complications, alternatives, and the limitations of current research.

If you have persistent vaginal discomfort, dryness, pain during intercourse, or urinary symptoms, the underlying cause should be evaluated rather than assuming that Votiva is the appropriate solution.



## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: What is the Votiva vaginal rejuvenation treatment?

Votiva is a radiofrequency-based treatment system developed by InMode. Depending on the handpiece and treatment plan, it may be used for selected internal or external tissue applications. Its use for vaginal rejuvenation should be evaluated in light of current clinical evidence and regulatory guidance.

### Q: Does Votiva use radiofrequency or laser technology?

Votiva uses radiofrequency technology, not laser energy. Different handpieces may deliver radiofrequency in different ways, so patients should ask which specific technology is being proposed.

### Q: How many Votiva sessions are needed?

The number of sessions varies according to the device configuration, provider's protocol, and individual circumstances. There is no universally established schedule that guarantees a particular result.

### Q: Is Votiva FDA-approved for vaginal rejuvenation?

Patients should not assume that Votiva or a particular handpiece is FDA-approved for vaginal rejuvenation. FDA clearance for a specific device indication does not establish approval for every vaginal or sexual-health application. Ask the provider to clarify the exact regulatory status and intended use.

### Q: Does Votiva guarantee improvements in vaginal tightness or sexual sensation?

No. Individual results cannot be guaranteed. Although some research has explored radiofrequency treatments for vulvovaginal concerns, the evidence has limitations, and more rigorous research is needed to establish long-term benefits for specific applications.

## Conclusion

Understanding the Votiva vaginal rejuvenation treatment involves more than knowing that it uses radiofrequency energy. Patients should understand the technology and handpieces, the distinction between internal and external applications, the individualized nature of treatment sessions, the limitations of clinical evidence, and the importance of regulatory and safety guidance.

Votiva should not be viewed as a guaranteed solution for vaginal laxity, dryness, sexual-function concerns, or urinary symptoms. A careful medical evaluation and an honest discussion of evidence, risks, and alternatives are essential before considering any energy-based vaginal procedure.
