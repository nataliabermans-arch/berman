Laser treatment for vaginal dryness is a non-surgical procedure that uses controlled laser energy to target vaginal tissue. It is marketed as an option for women experiencing vaginal dryness, irritation, and discomfort, particularly after menopause. The treatment is intended to stimulate tissue responses that may influence vaginal tissue structure and function.

Vaginal dryness can develop because of declining estrogen levels, menopause, breastfeeding, certain medications, and other hormonal changes. Although laser therapy has attracted attention as a non-hormonal approach, its clinical effectiveness remains uncertain. Research has investigated different laser technologies, but current medical guidelines do not support their routine use for treating vaginal dryness.

Understanding the proposed benefits, procedure, treatment sessions, possible risks, and available alternatives can help patients make informed decisions before considering laser treatment.

## What Is Laser Treatment for Vaginal Dryness?
Laser treatment for vaginal dryness is an energy-based procedure that delivers controlled laser energy to vaginal tissue. It is marketed to address vaginal dryness and discomfort, particularly in women experiencing menopausal changes.

Two commonly investigated technologies are:

- Fractional CO2 laser: Uses carbon dioxide laser energy to create controlled microscopic treatment zones in targeted tissue.
- Er:YAG laser: Uses erbium laser energy, which is strongly absorbed by water in tissue and produces controlled thermal effects.
These technologies are intended to stimulate tissue responses, including collagen remodeling and other healing-related processes. Researchers have investigated whether these effects can improve vaginal tissue characteristics and reduce symptoms associated with genitourinary syndrome of menopause (GSM).

However, the proposed biological effects do not establish that laser treatment reliably improves vaginal dryness or provides lasting relief.

### What Are the Benefits of Laser Treatment?
Laser treatment is marketed for potential improvements in vaginal dryness, irritation, and discomfort. However, clinical evidence has not established consistent benefits over sham procedures, and its effectiveness remains uncertain.

Potential benefits being investigated include:

- Vaginal dryness: Researchers have examined whether laser treatment can improve dryness associated with menopausal tissue changes.
- Vaginal irritation: Treatment has been investigated for its possible effects on discomfort and irritation.
- Sexual comfort: Some clinical studies have evaluated changes in pain during intercourse.
- Vaginal tissue changes: Researchers have investigated whether laser energy influences tissue structure.
- Non-hormonal treatment approach: Laser treatment does not involve administering estrogen or other hormonal medications.
These are potential treatment outcomes rather than guaranteed benefits.

According to [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34636862/), a randomized clinical trial published in 2021 investigated fractional CO2 laser treatment in 85 postmenopausal women. The study compared three laser sessions with sham treatment and found no significant difference in vaginal symptoms between the groups after 12 months. This finding highlights the uncertainty surrounding the treatment's effectiveness.

Women considering laser treatment should distinguish advertised benefits from improvements demonstrated through well-designed clinical research.

## What to Expect During the Procedure

Laser treatment for vaginal dryness generally involves applying controlled laser energy to selected vaginal tissue using a specialized medical device. The procedure usually begins with a medical assessment, followed by preparation and application of the laser.

During the procedure:

1. Initial consultation: The healthcare professional reviews vaginal symptoms, medical history, and possible causes of dryness.
2. Preparation: The treatment area is assessed, and the provider explains the procedure, potential discomfort, and possible complications.
3. Laser application: For internal treatment, a specialized applicator may be inserted into the vaginal canal to deliver controlled laser energy to the targeted tissue.
4. Completion: The applicator is removed, and the provider explains aftercare instructions and any temporary activity restrictions.
The experience can vary depending on the laser technology, treatment area, and provider's protocol. Patients should ask what sensations to expect and whether any preparation or recovery restrictions apply.

## How Many Sessions Will You Need?

There is no scientifically established number of laser sessions required to treat vaginal dryness. Some providers recommend multiple sessions, but the ideal number, frequency, and need for maintenance treatments have not been established through sufficient clinical evidence.

Published studies have investigated different treatment schedules. For example, a 2020 randomized, double-blind, sham-controlled study involving 88 postmenopausal women investigated three fractional CO2 laser sessions at four-week intervals.

The [study reported](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32345787/) improvements in certain vaginal health measurements after treatment. However, its results should not be interpreted as proof that three sessions are necessary or effective for every patient.

Treatment schedules may differ according to:

- The laser technology being used.
- The provider's treatment protocol.
- The patient's symptoms.
- The treatment area.
- The approach investigated in clinical research.

Patients should ask their healthcare professional to explain the evidence behind the recommended number of sessions rather than assuming that additional treatments will produce better results.

## Are There Any Risks or Side Effects?

Yes. Laser treatment for vaginal dryness can cause temporary discomfort, burning, irritation, spotting, and vaginal discharge. More serious complications, including vaginal burns and scarring, are also potential concerns.

Possible side effects include:

- Vaginal discomfort: Some patients may experience soreness during or after treatment.
- Burning sensation: Thermal energy may cause temporary burning or irritation.
- Spotting: Minor bleeding may occur following the procedure.
- Vaginal discharge: Some patients may notice changes in discharge.
- Tissue injury: Improper energy delivery may potentially damage vaginal tissue.
- Scarring: Tissue damage may result in scarring.
- Pain during intercourse: Pain may persist or develop following a procedure.

The exact frequency of these complications is not sufficiently established for vaginal laser treatment.

The [FDA warned](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices) about energy-based devices marketed for vaginal rejuvenation and related procedures. Its safety communication identified potential complications, including vaginal burns, scarring, pain during sexual intercourse, and recurring or chronic pain.

Patients should not assume that a non-surgical procedure is automatically risk-free. Understanding the potential complications is important before making a treatment decision.

## Is Laser Vaginal Therapy Right for You?

Laser vaginal therapy may be discussed with women experiencing persistent vaginal dryness, particularly after menopause. However, it is not an established first-line treatment, and being a potential candidate does not mean that laser therapy is medically recommended.

Women experiencing the following concerns may benefit from a medical evaluation:

- Persistent vaginal dryness.
- Vaginal irritation or discomfort.
- Pain during sexual intercourse.
- Vaginal symptoms associated with menopause.
- Symptoms that have not improved with initial management.

A healthcare professional should first identify the underlying cause of dryness and determine whether the symptoms are associated with GSM or another medical condition.

Patients with active vaginal infections, unexplained vaginal bleeding, or significant tissue irritation may require additional evaluation before any vaginal procedure is considered.

### Understanding Cost and Insurance

The cost of laser treatment for vaginal dryness varies depending on the clinic, laser technology, provider, and number of sessions recommended. Patients should request a complete cost estimate before agreeing to treatment.

Factors that may affect the overall expense include:

- Laser technology: Different laser devices and treatment systems may have different pricing.
- Number of sessions: Multiple appointments may increase the overall cost.
- Consultation fees: Initial medical assessments may involve additional charges.
- Provider fees: Costs can vary between healthcare professionals and clinics.
- Follow-up appointments: Additional consultations may contribute to the total expense.

Insurance coverage depends on the individual policy and medical indication. Cosmetic or elective vaginal rejuvenation procedures are generally not covered simply because they are marketed for vaginal dryness.

Before proceeding, patients should contact their insurance provider to confirm:

- Whether the proposed procedure is covered.
- Whether a medical diagnosis is required.
- Whether prior authorization is necessary.
- Whether consultation and follow-up fees are included.

Patients should not assume that insurance will cover laser treatment without receiving confirmation from their insurer.

## How Does Laser Therapy Compare to Other Treatments?

Laser therapy differs from established vaginal dryness treatments because it uses energy-based technology rather than moisturizers, lubricants, or medication. However, established treatments have stronger clinical support for managing vaginal dryness.

Common alternatives include:

- Vaginal moisturizers: Help maintain moisture and relieve ongoing vaginal dryness between applications.
- Vaginal lubricants: Reduce friction and discomfort during sexual activity but do not address underlying hormonal changes.
- Low-dose vaginal estrogen: Helps improve estrogen-related vaginal tissue changes and is an established treatment for appropriate patients.
- Vaginal DHEA: A prescription option for selected patients experiencing menopausal vaginal symptoms.
- Oral ospemifene: A prescription medication that acts on estrogen receptors and may help manage certain menopausal symptoms.
- Laser therapy: Uses controlled energy to target vaginal tissue, but its effectiveness for vaginal dryness remains uncertain.

According to the 2025 [AUA/SUFU/AUGS clinical guideline](file:///C:/Users/dell%20user/Downloads/GSM%20Unabridged%202025%20(1).pdf), available evidence does not support routine use of CO2 laser, Er:YAG laser, or radiofrequency for GSM-related vaginal dryness and discomfort. The guideline also emphasizes that CO2 laser should be considered experimental outside clinical trials, with appropriate discussion during shared decision-making.

The appropriate treatment depends on the underlying cause of dryness, symptom severity, medical history, and individual preferences.

Debunking Myths About Laser Treatment
Laser treatment for vaginal dryness is sometimes promoted with claims that go beyond what current clinical research establishes. Understanding these misconceptions can help patients make informed decisions.

- Myth: Laser treatment permanently eliminates vaginal dryness.

  Fact: There is no reliable clinical evidence establishing that vaginal laser treatment permanently eliminates dryness. Symptoms may continue because the underlying hormonal or medical cause remains.

- Myth: Laser treatment is completely risk-free.

  Fact: Possible complications include vaginal irritation, burns, scarring, and pain. Non-surgical procedures can still involve medical risks.

- Myth: Laser treatment is FDA-approved for vaginal dryness.

  Fact: FDA clearance or approval for a particular device or medical indication does not automatically establish authorization for vaginal rejuvenation or vaginal dryness treatment.

- Myth: Every woman experiencing menopausal dryness needs laser therapy.

  Fact: Many women can manage vaginal dryness with established treatments, including moisturizers, lubricants, and appropriate medical therapies.

- Myth: More laser sessions guarantee better results.

  Fact: No universally accepted treatment schedule has been established, and additional sessions do not guarantee improved outcomes.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: Does Laser Treatment Help With Vaginal Dryness?

Laser treatment is marketed to improve vaginal dryness by delivering controlled energy to vaginal tissue. However, current clinical evidence has not established consistent benefits over sham treatment or standard medical options.

### Q: Is CO2 Laser Treatment Effective for Vaginal Dryness?

CO2 laser treatment has been investigated in clinical studies involving women with menopausal vaginal dryness. Some studies have reported improvements, but the overall certainty of the evidence remains limited. Current clinical guidelines do not recommend its routine use.

### Q: How Many Laser Sessions Are Needed for Vaginal Dryness?

There is no scientifically established number of sessions required. Some clinical studies have investigated three sessions, but this does not establish a universal treatment schedule.

### Q: Can Laser Treatment Cause Vaginal Burns?

Yes. Vaginal burns are among the potential complications associated with energy-based vaginal procedures. Other possible complications include scarring and pain during sexual intercourse.

### Q: Is Laser Treatment Better Than Vaginal Estrogen?

There is insufficient evidence to establish that laser treatment is better than vaginal estrogen. Low-dose vaginal estrogen is an established treatment for appropriate patients experiencing GSM-related symptoms.

However, available clinical evidence has not established consistent benefits, and current medical guidelines do not support routine use of vaginal laser therapy for GSM-related dryness.
